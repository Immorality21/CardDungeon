### 2. Mobile hub: black columns on the left and right
- **Seen:** on mobile, the hub's background doesn't fill the screen; black bars fill both sides.
- **Wanted:** repaint the hub background **wider** so other aspect ratios show more or less of the painting, while the **buildable area stays the same**.
- **Approach (safe area plus bleed):**
  - Paint the image past today's 16:9, to about 21:9 or wider (phones held sideways are about 19.5:9 to 20:9).
  - Keep every building and clickable thing inside a fixed 16:9 box in the middle that never moves.
  - Scale so the safe box always fits fully on screen. Wider screens show more of the edges; narrower screens (16:10 or 4:3 tablets) leave space above and below, so think about adding some height to the image too.
  - The edges are scenery only (trees, walls, fog), because some players will never see them.
- **Area:** hub view (`Hub/UI/HubView`, `HubPresenter`), the hub background art (`docs/PIXEL_ART.md`); `docs/plans/HUB.md`
- **Status: done 2026-11-28, seen in the hub at 2400x1080 (20:9).** `hub_backdrop.png` is now 448x240: the old 320x180 in the middle, untouched apart from its smeared edge strips, plus 64 px of forest, road and clearing each side and 30 px of sky and ground above and below. Outpainted with PixelLab `inpaint_image` in 256x240 pieces (Tier 1 caps a side at 256): left and right, the top and bottom of the middle, then two passes over bands straddling the seams, since the new ground came out a shade darker than the old. 6 calls, 60 generations. `HubSO.BackdropSafeArea` (64,30,320,180) says which part is the town's box, and `HubView.PlaceBackdrop` hangs the rest past it, so buildings and hit boxes do not move. The stars spread over the wider sky (24, was 16). Left: a faint tone patch at the very bottom left, visible only on a 4:3 screen.
- **Then (owner, same day): the trees right of the road did not line up.** The outer edge of the right-hand seam band cut a straight vertical line through the treeline (x 394). One more pass over a 40 px strip across it, sky to forest floor, joined the trees up. 7 calls, 70 generations in all.

### 3. Sphere grid and dungeon map: panning has no limit
- **Seen:** on the sphere grid and the dungeon map you can pan away forever.
- **Wanted:** keep panning within bounds. Clamp it so the content can't leave the view, for example the content's edges can't go more than a margin past the middle of the view. Zoom obeys the same clamp, so zooming out near an edge can't strand you either.
- **Area:** `Heroes/UI/SphereGridView` (shared with the `Tools ▸ Sphere Grid Editor` window, so check the editor still behaves), and the dungeon map's pan/zoom
- **Status: built 2026-11-28, not yet seen in play.** `SphereGridView.BoundPan` (on by default) keeps the middle of the view over the graph's bounds after every pan, zoom and pinch, so at most half a screen of empty space shows past an edge. The story map uses the same view, so it is bounded too. Dragging back from an edge moves at once (the drag re-anchors on the clamped pan). Both editor windows turn it off. Test: `ClampedPan_KeepsTheViewCentreOverTheGraph`.

### 5. Level entry: a way to the Merchant, and potion icons
- **Where:** the screen before entering a level, where you see the party and pack potions. That is the level-entry screen (`Hub/PotionBeltPanel` on `progress-view`), not the campfire: potions moved there on 2026-11-27.

It shows only when the Merchant stands, the tutorial is finished and the level is not underway yet (once it is, the potions are packed).

### 6. Mobile combat: ability descriptions are unreadable
- **Seen:** on mobile, picking an ability (e.g. Aimed Shot) shows its description beside the list, but the box is narrower than a single word. The text breaks **inside words**: "one / enem / y: 19 / dama / ge".
- **Wanted:** shrink the **party panel**, which takes far more room than it needs, and give that width to the description. Also give the description a minimum width so words can never split.
- **Area:** the battle screen's UXML/USS (ability list, description, party panel); `docs/plans/POLISH_CONTENT.md` battle polish
- **Cause:** the picker was held to 36% of the screen (about 470px on a phone) and the list took a fixed 320px of that, so the description got about 90px.
- **Status: fixed 2026-11-28, seen in a sandbox at phone size** (2400×1080 with the 130% interface). The band's left window is 56% and the party 44%. The list takes 46% of the picker and the description the rest (about 390px on a phone), so Aimed Shot's line fits on one line. The party rows squeeze their name column and still fit.

### 9. Balance: non-replacing summons feel weaker than party-replacing ones
- **Feel:** summons that join beside the party seem generally weaker than summons that replace it.
- **Owner, 2026-11-28:** in play, the **replacing summons are game changers**: far more powerful, and they swing fights, especially when timed right. **Don't touch the replacing summons.** Only the non-replacing ones get buffed.
- **Wanted:** buff the non-replacing summons. Measure to size the buff, not to question the feel.
- **How to measure:** the encounter simulator already plays summons and their moves (`SummonMovePolicy`, `BALANCING.md` §5be) and the analyzer has summon sweeps. Run the same party against the same fights with each summon in turn and compare win rate, turns to win and HP lost, split into replacing and non-replacing. Account for what each type costs: a replacing summon takes the party off the field, and that is part of its price.
- **Known so far:** on 2026-11-13 the investment frontier found no summon that lowers any finale's ask; a summon build costs more than a breadth build (`NEXT_STEPS.md`, summons entry). That was measured over all summons together, not split by type.
- **Area:** `docs/BALANCING.md` (read first), `Balance/EncounterSimulator`, `SimAllies`, the summon assets; `docs/plans/SPECIALIZATION.md` §4 (summons)
- **Status: on the roadmap 2026-11-28** (`NEXT_STEPS.md`, Next up, first).

### 16. Surrender, from the pause menu in the dungeon
- **Wanted (owner):** a Surrender button in the in-dungeon options menu. It loses everything gained; nothing persists. Decided the same day: **the floor in progress** loses its XP, kill-gold and loot and the level ends, like a wipe; floors already cleared keep what they paid (each banked its rewards when cleared). Not before the first level has been cleared: the town has to be passed once first.
- **Status: done 2026-11-28, seen in play on a throwaway save.** "Surrender" sits above "Quit to Title"; the first press arms it ("Surrender: lose this floor?"), the second ends the level and loads the town (`RoomActionUI.OnSurrender`, `DungeonManager.HandleSurrender`). It shares the wipe's discard but pays no consolation Essence and counts no death (`ForfeitReason.Surrendered`; quests counting wipes ignore it). Run history records it as "Surrendered on floor N" (`RunOutcome.Surrendered`, tested). Hidden while no level is underway (the sandbox) and until the tutorial's first level is cleared. Checked: after a surrender `Run.json` is gone, Essence unchanged, the town loads.
- **Seen while testing:** the HUD names the first floor "Dungeon Entrance" under the level "Ruined Passage" (finding 1 left that floor name alone). Renamed to Ruined Passage the same day.
